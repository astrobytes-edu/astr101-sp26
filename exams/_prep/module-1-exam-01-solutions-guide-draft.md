## Lecture 1 (Q01-Q02)

### Q01
An astronomer reports that a star's spectral line has shifted slightly to longer wavelength over two nights. Which quantity was directly measured?

A. The star's radial velocity  
B. The star's mass  
C. The wavelength shift of the line  
D. The star's temperature

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** The direct observable is the line's wavelength shift. Radial velocity, mass, and temperature are inferred from models and additional assumptions.

### Q02
Why do standard candles matter in astronomy?

A. They provide a direct measurement of stellar mass from gravity alone.  
B. They let us infer distance by comparing known luminosity to observed brightness.  
C. They remove all uncertainty in the inverse-square law.  
D. They make Doppler shifts easier to detect.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** If luminosity is known, observed flux gives distance via inverse-square scaling.

## Lecture 2 (Q03-Q05)

### Q03
Two identical stars are observed. Star B is 4 times farther away than Star A. How bright does Star B appear compared with Star A?

A. 1/2 as bright  
B. 1/4 as bright  
C. 1/8 as bright  
D. 1/16 as bright

**Solution: D**  
**Reasoning:** Brightness scales as \(1/d^2\). A 4x increase in distance gives \(1/4^2 = 1/16\).

### Q04
A probe speed is 0.50 km/s. What is this in m/s?

A. 50 m/s  
B. 500 m/s  
C. 5,000 m/s  
D. 0.5 m/s

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(1\ \mathrm{km/s} = 1000\ \mathrm{m/s}\), so \(0.50\ \mathrm{km/s} = 500\ \mathrm{m/s}\).

### Q05
Which equation is dimensionally valid for speed?

A. \(v = d/t\)  
B. \(v = d \cdot t\)  
C. \(v = t/d\)  
D. \(v = d + t\)

**Solution: A**  
**Reasoning:** Speed has units of length divided by time.

## Lecture 3 (Q06-Q08)

### Q06
Australia has summer in December while the Northern Hemisphere has winter. This best supports which explanation of seasons?

A. Earth is farther from the Sun in Northern Hemisphere winter, so both hemispheres cool together.  
B. Earth's axial tilt changes Sun angle and daylight length differently by hemisphere.  
C. Seasons are caused by periodic changes in the Sun's luminosity.  
D. Seasons are caused by the Moon's changing phase.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Opposite seasons in opposite hemispheres are explained by tilt geometry, not distance.

### Q07
A satellite appears 2.0 arcminutes wide at distance \(d\). If distance becomes \(3d\), what is its angular size?

A. 6.0 arcminutes  
B. 2.0 arcminutes  
C. 0.67 arcminutes  
D. 0.22 arcminutes

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Angular size is inversely proportional to distance, so \(2.0/3 \approx 0.67\) arcmin.

### Q08
Which statement about constellations is most accurate?

A. Stars in a constellation are usually at the same distance from Earth.  
B. Constellations are physical star clusters bound by gravity.  
C. Constellations are line-of-sight patterns; stars can be at very different distances.  
D. Constellations are caused by Earth's shadow on distant stars.

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Constellations are projection effects on the sky, not usually physical groupings.

## Lecture 4 (Q09-Q11)

### Q09
Which statement correctly distinguishes phases from eclipses?

A. Phases are caused by Earth's shadow; eclipses are caused by reflected sunlight.  
B. Phases are due to changing Sun-Earth-Moon geometry; eclipses need special alignment near nodes.  
C. Phases and eclipses are the same event observed from different places.  
D. Phases occur only when the Moon is closest to Earth.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Phases are monthly illumination geometry; eclipses require near-node alignment.

### Q10
A first-quarter Moon is highest in the sky around:

A. Sunrise  
B. Noon  
C. Sunset  
D. Midnight

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** First-quarter is about 90 degrees east of the Sun, so it is high around sunset.

### Q11
Why is there not a solar eclipse at every new Moon?

A. The Moon only reflects enough light for eclipses in summer.  
B. The Moon's orbit is tilted, so most new Moons miss exact node alignment.  
C. Earth's shadow is too small most months.  
D. Solar eclipses require a full Moon, not a new Moon.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Orbital tilt (\(\sim 5^\circ\)) prevents perfect monthly alignment.

## Lecture 5 (Q12-Q14)

### Q12
Which statement best captures "Kepler vs Newton"?

A. Kepler explained gravity; Newton found only observational patterns.  
B. Kepler found empirical orbital patterns; Newton explained them with gravity.  
C. Both Kepler and Newton gave only geometric descriptions with no mechanism.  
D. Newton's laws apply only to the Solar System, Kepler's apply universally.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Kepler described what planets do; Newton explained why via dynamics and gravity.

### Q13
A planet orbits a Sun-like star at 4 AU. Using ratio reasoning with Kepler's third law, its period is:

A. 4 times Earth's year  
B. 8 times Earth's year  
C. 16 times Earth's year  
D. 64 times Earth's year

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(P \propto a^{3/2}\), so \(4^{3/2} = 8\).

### Q14
Retrograde motion of Mars in the heliocentric model is best explained by:

A. Mars physically reversing its orbital direction.  
B. Earth overtaking Mars, changing our line-of-sight projection.  
C. Temporary failure of Newton's laws.  
D. Earth's axial precession over a few weeks.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Retrograde is apparent motion from relative orbital geometry.

## Lecture 6 (Q15-Q17)

### Q15
What is the weight of a 70 kg person on the Moon (\(g \approx 1.6\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\))?

A. 70 N  
B. 112 N  
C. 686 N  
D. 44 N

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(W = mg = 70 \times 1.6 = 112\ \mathrm{N}\).

### Q16
If you move from distance \(R\) to \(2R\) from a planet's center, gravitational force becomes:

A. 2 times as strong  
B. 1/2 as strong  
C. 1/4 as strong  
D. unchanged

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Inverse-square law gives \(F \propto 1/r^2\), so doubling distance gives \(1/4\).

### Q17
Why does the shorthand \(P^2 = a^3\) not work unchanged for every orbiting system?

A. It fails because gravity is not inverse-square outside the Solar System.  
B. It is a special unit/central-mass form; general form includes \(M\) and constants.  
C. It works only for circular orbits and fails for all ellipses.  
D. It requires no central mass at all.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** The shorthand is a special-case normalization; the general Newtonian form depends on central mass.

## Lecture 7 (Q18-Q19)

### Q18
Which ordering is correct from longest to shortest wavelength?

A. Gamma, X-ray, UV, visible, IR, radio  
B. Radio, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma  
C. IR, radio, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma  
D. Visible, UV, IR, radio, X-ray, gamma

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Radio waves are longest and gamma rays shortest.

### Q19
Rayleigh scattering scales as \(1/\lambda^4\). Compared to 800 nm light, 400 nm light scatters:

A. 2 times more  
B. 4 times more  
C. 8 times more  
D. 16 times more

**Solution: D**  
**Reasoning:** \((800/400)^4 = 2^4 = 16\).

## Lecture 8 (Q20-Q21)

### Q20
Star A peaks at 500 nm and Star B peaks at 1000 nm. Using Wien's law, \(T_A/T_B\) is:

A. 1/2  
B. 1  
C. 2  
D. 4

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Wien gives \(T \propto 1/\lambda\), so \(T_A/T_B = 1000/500 = 2\).

### Q21
Two stars have equal radius. If Star X is twice as hot as Star Y, \(L_X/L_Y\) is:

A. 2  
B. 4  
C. 8  
D. 16

**Solution: D**  
**Reasoning:** \(L \propto T^4\) at fixed radius, so \(2^4 = 16\).

## Lecture 9 (Q22-Q24)

### Q22
A hot, low-density gas cloud with no bright background source produces mainly:

A. Continuous spectrum  
B. Absorption-line spectrum  
C. Emission-line spectrum  
D. No spectrum

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Hot, thin gas emits at discrete line wavelengths.

### Q23
Why are Balmer hydrogen lines not strongest in the hottest O stars?

A. O stars have no hydrogen.  
B. Hydrogen is mostly ionized, reducing \(n=2\) absorbers.  
C. O stars emit only radio wavelengths.  
D. Balmer lines require molecules, not atoms.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Extreme temperatures ionize hydrogen, so fewer atoms are in states that create strong Balmer absorption.

### Q24
A photon has \(E = 2.48\ \mathrm{eV}\). Using \(\lambda(\mathrm{nm}) = 1240/E(\mathrm{eV})\), wavelength is about:

A. 250 nm  
B. 500 nm  
C. 750 nm  
D. 1000 nm

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(1240/2.48 \approx 500\ \mathrm{nm}\).

## Lecture 10 (Q25-Q30)

### Q25
A line shifts from 589.0 nm to 589.2 nm. Approximate radial speed is:

A. \(10\ \mathrm{km/s}\), approaching  
B. \(100\ \mathrm{km/s}\), receding  
C. \(1000\ \mathrm{km/s}\), approaching  
D. \(0.1\ \mathrm{km/s}\), receding

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(v/c \approx \Delta\lambda/\lambda = 0.2/589\), giving about \(100\ \mathrm{km/s}\), and redshift means receding.

### Q26
A star moves purely across the sky (no line-of-sight component). Observed Doppler shift is:

A. Strong blueshift  
B. Strong redshift  
C. Zero Doppler shift  
D. Alternating red/blue each day

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Doppler shift measures radial velocity only.

### Q27
Telescope A has diameter 4 m, Telescope B has 2 m. Compared to B, A has:

A. 2 times light-gathering, 2 times better resolution  
B. 4 times light-gathering, 2 times better resolution  
C. 4 times light-gathering, 4 times better resolution  
D. 2 times light-gathering, same resolution

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Light-gathering \(\propto D^2\) gives \(4\times\); resolution \(\propto 1/D\) gives \(2\times\) finer.

### Q28
Flat galaxy rotation curves imply:

A. Most mass is concentrated only at the luminous center.  
B. Outer stars orbit too slowly, so gravity is weaker than expected.  
C. Outer stars orbit too fast for visible mass alone, indicating extra unseen mass.  
D. Doppler shifts cannot measure galactic motion.

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Measured orbital speeds stay high at large radius, requiring additional non-luminous mass.

### Q29
A line at 500.000 nm oscillates by \(\pm 0.005\) nm. Radial velocity amplitude is closest to:

A. \(0.3\ \mathrm{km/s}\)  
B. \(3\ \mathrm{km/s}\)  
C. \(30\ \mathrm{km/s}\)  
D. \(300\ \mathrm{km/s}\)

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** \(v \approx c(\Delta\lambda/\lambda) = 3\times10^5(0.005/500) \approx 3\ \mathrm{km/s}\).

### Q30
Why is space especially important for infrared astronomy?

A. Infrared does not obey diffraction.  
B. Earth's atmosphere both absorbs and emits infrared strongly.  
C. Mirrors in space are always larger than ground mirrors.  
D. Doppler shifts only work outside Earth's atmosphere.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Ground observations suffer from atmospheric absorption and thermal background in IR.

## Lecture 11 (Q31-Q35)

### Q31
Which architecture matches the standard Solar System pattern?

A. Gas giants inside 1 AU, rocky planets beyond 10 AU  
B. Rocky planets inside, gas/ice giants farther out  
C. Planet types are random with distance  
D. All planets formed with identical composition

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** This pattern follows condensation physics and frost-line structure.

### Q32
If stellar luminosity during formation were 4 times larger, the frost line distance would be about:

A. 1/4 as far  
B. 1/2 as far  
C. 2 times farther  
D. 4 times farther

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Characteristic temperature-distance boundaries scale roughly with \(\sqrt{L}\), so factor 2 farther.

### Q33
To determine whether an exoplanet is rocky or gaseous, the most direct pair of measured properties is:

A. Color and orbital inclination  
B. Mass and radius  
C. Rotation period and albedo  
D. Distance and age

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Mass plus radius gives density, a first-order composition diagnostic.

### Q34
Planet P has \(M = 8M_\oplus\) and \(R = 2R_\oplus\). Relative to Earth, its average density is:

A. 1/4 Earth density  
B. 1/2 Earth density  
C. About equal to Earth density  
D. 4 times Earth density

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** \(\rho \propto M/R^3\), so \(8/2^3 = 8/8 = 1\), about Earth-like.

### Q35
Why do most planets orbit in nearly the same plane and direction?

A. Tidal locking forces all orbital planes to match.  
B. They formed from a rotating, flattened protoplanetary disk.  
C. Interstellar winds align orbits after formation.  
D. Kepler's second law requires coplanar motion.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Shared disk origin naturally yields common direction and near-coplanarity.

## Lecture 12 (Q36-Q40)

### Q36
Transit depth scales as \((R_p/R_*)^2\). A \(2R_\oplus\) planet around a Sun-like star has depth relative to Earth-like transit depth:

A. 2 times deeper  
B. 4 times deeper  
C. 8 times deeper  
D. 16 times deeper

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Doubling radius quadruples area ratio and therefore transit depth.

### Q37
Detecting both transit and RV for the same planet lets you determine:

A. Radius and density directly, but not mass  
B. Mass and radius, hence average density  
C. Composition exactly, without uncertainty  
D. Atmospheric biosignatures directly from RV

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Transit constrains size, RV constrains mass; together they yield bulk density.

### Q38
If a star has luminosity \(0.25L_\odot\), the characteristic habitable-zone distance scales to:

A. 0.25 AU  
B. 0.50 AU  
C. 2.0 AU  
D. 4.0 AU

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Habitable-zone distance scales approximately as \(\sqrt{L}\), so \(\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\).

### Q39
If Bond albedo increases from 0.30 to 0.50, equilibrium temperature should:

A. Increase strongly, because more light is reflected  
B. Stay exactly the same  
C. Decrease, because less sunlight is absorbed  
D. Decrease only if orbital period changes

**Solution: C**  
**Reasoning:** Higher albedo means less absorbed energy, lowering equilibrium temperature.

### Q40
Which statement is scientifically strongest?

A. Any planet in the habitable zone must host life.  
B. Habitable zone location is a useful start, but atmosphere and geology still matter.  
C. A planet outside the habitable zone can never have liquid water anywhere.  
D. Biosignatures are unnecessary if orbit lies in the habitable zone.

**Solution: B**  
**Reasoning:** Habitable zone is a screening criterion, not a guarantee of habitability.
