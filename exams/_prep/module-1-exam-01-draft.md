## Answer Key (Draft) with One-Line Rationales

1. C - Wavelength shift is observed directly; velocity is inferred from a model.  
2. B - Standard candles link intrinsic luminosity to apparent brightness for distance inference.  
3. D - Brightness follows inverse-square, so \(4^2 = 16\) times fainter.  
4. B - \(0.50\ \mathrm{km/s} = 500\ \mathrm{m/s}\).  
5. A - Speed has units of length per time.  
6. B - Opposite seasons by hemisphere support axial tilt geometry.  
7. C - Angular size scales inversely with distance, so \(2/3\) arcmin.  
8. C - Constellations are projection patterns, not usually physical groups.  
9. B - Phases are illumination geometry; eclipses need node alignment.  
10. C - First-quarter Moon is about 90 degrees east of Sun, highest near sunset.  
11. B - Orbital tilt means most new/full Moons are off-node.  
12. B - Kepler described data patterns; Newton provided physical mechanism.  
13. B - \(P \propto a^{3/2}\), so \(4^{3/2}=8\).  
14. B - Retrograde is apparent, caused by relative orbital motion and viewing geometry.  
15. B - \(W=mg=70\times1.6=112\ \mathrm{N}\).  
16. C - Inverse-square gives \(1/2^2 = 1/4\).  
17. B - Full law depends on central mass and constants; shorthand is unit-specific.  
18. B - Radio has longest wavelength; gamma has shortest.  
19. D - \((800/400)^4 = 2^4 = 16\).  
20. C - Wien implies \(T\propto1/\lambda\), so \(1000/500=2\).  
21. D - Stefan-Boltzmann gives \(L\propto T^4\), so \(2^4=16\).  
22. C - Hot, thin gas produces emission lines.  
23. B - Extreme temperatures ionize hydrogen, weakening Balmer absorption.  
24. B - \(1240/2.48 \approx 500\ \mathrm{nm}\).  
25. B - \(v/c \approx 0.2/589\), so \(v\approx100\ \mathrm{km/s}\), redshift means receding.  
26. C - Doppler measures only radial component.  
27. B - Area scales \(D^2\) (4x), resolution scales \(1/D\) (2x finer).  
28. C - Flat curves require additional mass beyond luminous matter.  
29. B - \(v \approx 3\times10^5 \times (0.005/500) = 3\ \mathrm{km/s}\).  
30. B - Atmosphere blocks and glows in IR, degrading sensitivity from ground.  
31. B - Inner rocky plus outer giant planets is the observed pattern.  
32. C - Frost-line distance scales roughly as \(\sqrt{L}\), so factor 2 farther.  
33. B - Mass and radius give density, a key composition discriminator.  
34. C - Density scales as \(M/R^3=8/8=1\), about Earth-like.  
35. B - Shared plane/direction reflects origin in one rotating disk.  
36. B - Transit depth scales with radius squared, so \(2^2=4\).  
37. B - Transit gives radius; RV gives mass, together yield density.  
38. B - HZ distance scales as \(\sqrt{0.25}=0.5\).  
39. C - Higher albedo means less absorbed energy, lowering equilibrium temperature.  
40. B - HZ is necessary-not-sufficient; atmosphere and feedbacks still control habitability.
