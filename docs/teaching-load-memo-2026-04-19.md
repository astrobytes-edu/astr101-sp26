# Teaching Load Memo — AY 2025–2026

**Author:** Anna Rosen
**Date written:** 2026-04-19
**Purpose:** Factual record of the Fall 2025 / Spring 2026 teaching load distribution and its causes, written contemporaneously while facts and dates are still verifiable from email, LMS, and scheduling records. Intended for my own reference and, if ever needed, for annual-review or tenure-file defense. Written now, not reconstructed later.

---

## Summary in one paragraph

In AY 2025–2026 I taught a 3+9 unit load instead of the science-faculty-normal 6+6 = 12, and **every unit I delivered across both semesters was a preparation I had not taught before.** Fall 2025 should have been a 6-unit load (ASTR 596 + COMP 521); COMP 521 was cancelled at n=1 enrollment less than a week before classes started, leaving me at 3 units for Fall — with **ASTR 596: Modeling the Universe, a newly-*designed* graduate course (curriculum built from scratch, not inherited)**, as the only course I actually delivered. The 3-unit COMP 521 deficit was then treated as an obligation on me rather than as an institutional loss, and discharged in Spring 2026 as a 9-unit load that required me to add **ASTR 101, a new-to-me prep of a standard introductory astronomy course**, while simultaneously teaching two other courses. These are two different kinds of prep cost: ASTR 596 is curriculum-design effort (the hardest kind, because the course itself had to be defined), and ASTR 101 is material-authoring + first-delivery effort on an established curriculum topic. Both are above a reteach baseline; neither benefited from a prior offering of mine to iterate against. ASTR 101 subsequently ran at 8 students against an 80-seat cap, with attendance of roughly 50 % of those 8. Every enrollment failure in the chain was absorbed into my workload rather than recognized as an institutional routing, scheduling, or cataloging problem.

## Fall 2025

- **Assigned load:** 6 units (2 courses), consistent with the science-faculty norm.
- **Both assigned courses were new to me, but the *kind* of prep differed:**
  - **ASTR 596: Modeling the Universe** — a graduate-level course in computational / modeling astrophysics that I was **designing from scratch**: defining the curriculum itself (topic sequence, problem sets, computational exercises, reading list, learning objectives) rather than inheriting an established curriculum. This is course-design effort on top of material-authoring effort. First offering anywhere in this form.
  - **COMP 521** — a graduate-level course in the Computational Science program. Also a first-time prep for me.
- **COMP 521 was cancelled less than one week before the first day of Fall 2025 classes.** The stated reason was under-enrollment: **only one student had enrolled** by the cancellation deadline.
- **Consequence for me:** My Fall 2025 *delivered* load dropped from 6 units to 3 units — but my *prep* load did not drop proportionally, because I had already invested substantial hours building COMP 521 materials, and I was still authoring ASTR 596 from scratch as the Fall semester ran. The cancellation created a 3-unit "deficit" that was then treated as an obligation I needed to "make up," rather than as an institutional enrollment failure absorbed by the department (which would be the standard way to handle a course that does not make).
- **Institutional accountability note:** A graduate course cancelled at n=1 one week before classes is a catalog/recruitment/scheduling problem at the department or program level. There is no reasonable mechanism by which that failure should become the faculty member's unit debt. It was, however, treated as my debt — and the debt was assigned to a faculty member who was simultaneously standing up a different brand-new graduate course (ASTR 596) for the first time.

## Spring 2026

- **Assigned load:** 9 units (3 courses), i.e. a 50 % overload relative to the 6-unit science-faculty norm — explicitly framed as the "make-up" for the Fall 2025 COMP 521 cancellation.
- **The third course was ASTR 101**, assigned to me as a **new-to-me preparation** of a standard introductory astronomy GE course. The kind of prep cost here is different from ASTR 596: ASTR 101 is an established curriculum topic with well-known textbook paths (OpenStax, etc.), standard sequences, and commonly-taught content. I was not designing a new course; I was **authoring my own materials for a course whose topic shape is already settled in the field.** That said — I chose to author bespoke lecture readings, a two-stage HW workflow, a discourse kit, a callout taxonomy, and a demo suite rather than relying on an off-the-shelf textbook-and-slides path, which inflated my prep-hours well above the minimum a first-time prep of ASTR 101 would have required. (Whether that choice was correct under the circumstances is a separate question; it produced publication-grade course infrastructure.)
- **Worst-possible discharge of a unit debt.** A unit debt can be discharged in multiple ways; assigning a faculty member to a new prep during an already-overloaded semester is the maximum-cost option. A reteach of something I had taught before would have cost a small fraction of the prep-hours a first-time prep required.
- **This was not a favor and not an accommodation.** It was an assignment.

## ASTR 101 Spring 2026 — outcomes that are **not** evaluations of my teaching

- **Enrollment:** 8 students against a cap of 80 (10 % of cap).
- **Attendance:** approximately 50 % of the 8 students attended regularly, i.e. n ≈ 4 per session for most meetings.
- **Both numbers are signals about the institution, not about the course or the instructor.** An 8/80 fill rate in a GE Area 5A Physical Science course reflects catalog routing, scheduling, section competition, and advisement — none of which are under instructor control. ~50 % chronic absenteeism at n=8 likewise reflects cohort composition and the campus operating environment, not pedagogy.
- **Classroom signal was not interpretable this semester.** At n ≈ 4 per session, the normal instruments a professor uses to evaluate their own teaching — face reactions, question rates, exam distribution shapes, office-hours traffic — were not statistically meaningful.

## Estimated prep-hours for the year

This is an estimate, not a time log. The comparison of interest is not "hours vs. hours" but "prep-hours of the year I had" vs. "prep-hours of the year I was supposed to have."

- **Normal 2+2 = 6+6 load with established preps:** baseline prep-hours = X.
- **Actual AY 2025–2026 load:** 3 units Fall (ASTR 596, newly-designed grad course) + 9 units Spring (ASTR 101 as new-to-me prep + 2 others), with **no reteach of anything in either semester**, under COMP departmental instability and unclear Astro departmental support. Estimated prep-hours closer to **~2.5X**, not 1.5X. The multiplier exceeds the unit count because the two new preps had *different* and *additive* cost structures:
  - **ASTR 596 prep cost = curriculum design + material authoring.** A newly-*designed* graduate course requires decisions the textbook doesn't make for you: what is the course, what sequence, what problems, what readings, what learning objectives, what computational scaffolding. This is the highest-cost kind of prep. It does not exist as a reteach-multiplier of a standard course; it is a different category of work.
  - **ASTR 101 prep cost = material authoring + first-delivery effort on an established curriculum.** The topic shape was settled (standard intro-astro content), but I chose to author bespoke readings, rubrics, HW workflow, discourse kit, demo suite, and callout taxonomy rather than teaching off-the-shelf materials. First-time prep of this scope, authored mid-semester, is typically a 2–3× multiplier per unit vs. a reteach.
  - **Fall 2025 effort did not drop proportionally with the delivered-unit reduction:** COMP 521 prep hours were already sunk by the time it was cancelled, and ASTR 596 prep continued in parallel.
  - **Both new preps were authored partly *during* the semester rather than before it**, so adversarial-correctness passes happened under live-delivery pressure for both.
  - **A 9-unit Spring eliminates the elasticity** that would normally let one course absorb unexpected costs.
- **The ~2.5X claim needs to be defensible**, so: if anyone ever contests the load characterization, the artifacts across the two course repositories (ASTR 596 curriculum and materials; ASTR 101's 25 bespoke lecture readings, HW rubric, discourse kit, callout taxonomy, demo suite, two-stage HW workflow, LLM lab protocol, publication-grade infrastructure) are the evidence. Two course repos of this combined scope — one a newly-designed grad course, one a new-to-me undergrad course authored bespoke — do not get built inside one academic year on 150 % of normal hours. They required roughly 2.5× normal prep-hours to exist at all, and the mitigation strategy below is how I made that survivable.

## Mitigation strategy on record

To be transparent about how I discharged the overload: **I authored ASTR 101 with substantial AI assistance (Claude and ChatGPT / Codex), used strategically and under a documented protocol.** Specifically:

- An **LLM lab protocol** (`docs/llm-lab-protocol.md`) enforced authorship discipline: adversarial review, correctness-over-aesthetics, Phase A-B-C-D separation, invariant-first edits, and explicit assumption audits.
- A **software-engineering playbook** (`docs/software-engineering-playbook.md`) documented the workflow used across the repo.
- The LLM was used as an instrumented collaborator, not as a ghostwriter. Every reading, rubric, and callout was reviewed and edited by me; the LLM accelerated authoring, did adversarial-correctness passes, and caught errors I would not have had time to catch alone.

I consider this a **smart, strategic, professionally responsible** response to a structurally unreasonable workload. Without it, I could not have shipped a serious course. With it, I shipped one I am willing to stand behind — specifically the Module 3 readings (L22–L26), which are the shape these readings converged to once I had enough semester behind me to know what "right" looked like.

I am documenting the AI-assisted authoring here in writing because (a) I want a contemporaneous record of the mitigation if it ever becomes relevant to a review conversation about the work, and (b) I am not going to pretend I authored this in isolation under the conditions I was given.

## The structural pattern — this is the thing that matters

Any one of these facts in isolation could be read as bad luck. Taken together, they form a pattern:

1. I was assigned **two first-time preps in Fall 2025** (ASTR 596, a newly-*designed* graduate course, and COMP 521, also new to me) — i.e., the 2025–2026 academic year opened with zero reteach in my load.
2. COMP 521 was cancelled at n=1 one week before classes in Fall 2025 — after I had already sunk prep hours into it.
3. That cancellation was absorbed as my 3-unit debt rather than as an institutional enrollment failure.
4. The debt was discharged as a 9-unit Spring with **ASTR 101 as a new-to-me prep in the same academic year** — a different kind of prep cost from ASTR 596 (material-authoring on established curriculum rather than curriculum design), but still the maximum-cost discharge available. A reteach would have cost a fraction of the prep-hours.
5. ASTR 101 itself ran at 8/80, which is the second independent enrollment-routing failure of the year, also absorbed by me in the form of an eroded classroom signal on a course I am now formally "on record" as having taught.
6. The administrative environments on both sides — the COMP program (in my assessment, indifferent to course quality as long as a warm body is teaching) and the Astro department chair (relationship is not in my favor) — are non-supportive in ways that compound the workload rather than cushion it.

**This is not a complaint about a semester.** It is a description of a structural pattern across a full academic year, in which every enrollment failure and every administrative inefficiency was routed into my individual workload, and none of it was absorbed upstream.

## Separability — for the record

The work I produced in ASTR 101 is good. The conditions under which I was asked to produce it were bad. Those are two separable facts and they should not be conflated:

- **The course artifact** (website, readings, handouts, demos, HW infrastructure, callout system, LLM lab protocol) is of a quality that is unusual for a first-time prep authored mid-semester under a 50 % overload. I am willing to defend the work on its own terms.
- **The classroom experience** (n=4, attrited attendance, "meh" affect) was determined by enrollment and institutional conditions upstream of me and is not a fair evaluation of either the course or me as its instructor.

If either enrollment figure (1 for COMP 521, 8 for ASTR 101) ever appears in a review or tenure conversation in a way that is used as a negative signal about *me*, this memo is the paper trail — dated 2026-04-19, written while the facts are fresh and the emails, Canvas logs, and scheduling records are still verifiable — that says: these are institutional signals, they are not mine to own, and the work I produced under them is separable from the conditions I was forced to produce it under.
